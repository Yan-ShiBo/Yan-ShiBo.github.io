Research

Safety and verifiability in uncertain control systems.

Autonomous systems increasingly rely on learning-enabled policies, yet disturbance, model mismatch, and unseen conditions mean that successful experiments alone do not establish reliability. My research examines how task boundaries, risk, and the scope of a claim can be stated clearly.

Research background Research directions
Background Core question Significance Directions
Background

Training performance alone cannot establish reliability in real systems.

Data-driven and learning-enabled controllers are increasingly used in complex dynamical systems. They can learn effective behavior from data, but finite training and simulation cannot cover every state, disturbance, or abnormal condition.

Uncertainty in real environments

Stochastic disturbances, model mismatch, sensor noise, and environmental change all affect behavior. For safety-critical tasks, average performance alone does not explain rare or extreme conditions.

Safety claims beyond experiments

Training curves and simulations show how a policy behaves in observed scenarios, but they cannot exhaust the state space. Research must therefore ask which safety claims can be checked and reproduced under explicit assumptions.

Core question

Task completion and risk both need explicit treatment.

For tasks that require reaching a goal while avoiding unsafe regions, a safety claim should state the objective, the risk, and the conditions under which the claim applies.

Task and boundary

Initial, target, and unsafe regions state what the system should accomplish and which behaviors are unacceptable.

Stochastic environment

When disturbances are unavoidable, safety and reachability must be expressed probabilistically rather than assuming every run is identical.

Checkable conclusion

The goal is formal evidence that can be reviewed and that states the assumptions and scope on which a conclusion depends.

Why it matters

Making safety requirements discussable, comparable, and reviewable during system design.

Experiments show how a system performs in observed settings; research on trustworthiness also states the assumptions, risk boundaries, and scope on which a conclusion depends.

More trustworthy autonomy

In transportation, robotics, and other cyber-physical systems, safety requirements should be addressed during system design rather than explained only after a failure.

Clearer boundaries for claims

A formal statement exposes the assumptions and scope of a guarantee, making research conclusions easier to compare, review, and extend.

Long-term capability for complex systems

The research is not only about one algorithm; it also asks whether verification ideas can adapt to nonlinear, higher-dimensional, and model-uncertain systems.

Research directions

Current areas of interest.

These directions share a long-term question: how to consider control performance, safety requirements, and verifiability together in uncertain environments.

Trustworthy control for safety-critical intelligent systems

Study the reliability of learning-enabled control in safety-critical settings so that policy performance and checkable safety requirements can be considered together.

Probabilistic safety and reachability for stochastic systems

Study how to express and analyze probabilistic guarantees when a system must reach a target region while continuing to avoid unsafe regions under stochastic disturbances.

Formal verification of learning-enabled control policies

Connect the adaptability of data-driven control with the auditability of formal methods to understand why a policy is reliable under stated conditions.

Scalable verification for complex systems

Explore verification ideas that can extend to nonlinear, higher-dimensional, and model-uncertain systems and more complex tasks.
